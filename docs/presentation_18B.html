1. The Cage Effect

Gas Phase:
Hit and Run. A collision lasts $\sim 10^{-13}$ s.
Liquid Phase:
Trapped! Solvents form a "cage". Partners collide $100\times$ before escaping.

2. Two-Step Mechanism

Reaction requires diffusion into the cage, then chemistry.

$$A + B \underset{k_{-d} \text{ (Escape)}}{\overset{k_d \text{ (Entry)}}{\rightleftharpoons}} [AB] \xrightarrow{k_a \text{ (React)}} P$$

Limit 1: Diffusion Control

Chemistry is fast ($k_a \gg k_{-d}$).

Rate depends on how fast they meet.

High Viscosity, Low Activation Energy

Limit 2: Activation Control

Chemistry is slow ($k_a \ll k_{-d}$).

Rate depends on barrier height.

Low Viscosity, High Activation Energy

3. The Smoluchowski Limit

What is the physical speed limit of chemistry?

If every encounter leads to reaction immediately:

$$k_{max} = 4 \pi (R_A + R_B) (D_A + D_B) N_A$$

Combining with Stokes-Einstein ($D \sim 1/r$)...

Derivation Below

Derivation: Stokes-Einstein

Substitute $D = \frac{k_B T}{6\pi \eta r}$:

$$k_{max} \approx \frac{8 R T}{3 \eta}$$

Key Insight: Rate is independent of molecular size!

  • Big molecules move slow (small D), but are big targets (large R).
  • The effects cancel out.
