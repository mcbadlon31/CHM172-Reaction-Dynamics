Crossing the Energy Barrier

1. The Activated Complex

Often called the Transition State (TS).

$$A + B \underset{K^\ddagger}{\rightleftharpoons} [AB]^\ddagger \xrightarrow{k^\ddagger} P$$

Properties:

  • Located at the barrier peak (saddle point).
  • Quasi-equilibrium with reactants.
  • Vibrational frequency $\nu^\ddagger \to$ Transition.
Myth: "Intermediate"
Reality: Lifetime $\approx 0$ (one vibration).

2. The Eyring Equation

Connecting Thermodynamics to Kinetics.

$$k = \frac{k_B T}{h} \cdot e^{\Delta^\ddagger S^\circ / R} \cdot e^{-\Delta^\ddagger H^\circ / RT}$$

$\Delta^\ddagger H^\circ$ (Enthalpy)

Energy Barrier

Roughly equal to Arrhenius $E_a$.

$\Delta^\ddagger S^\circ$ (Entropy)

Probability/Ordering

Determines the $A$-factor.

  • $\Delta S < 0$: Tight TS (Associative)
  • $\Delta S > 0$: Loose TS (Dissociative)

Eyring Plot Explorer

Linear Form: $\ln(k/T)$ vs $1/T$.

Slope $= -\Delta^\ddagger H^\circ / R$
Intercept $\propto \Delta^\ddagger S^\circ$

3. Kinetic Isotope Effect (KIE)

$$\frac{k_H}{k_D} \approx 7 \quad (\text{at } 298 \text{ K})$$

Replacing H with D slows the reaction.

Cause: Zero Point Energy (ZPE)

C-D bonds are lower in energy than C-H bonds. They have a higher "effective barrier" to climb.

ZPE Difference $\approx \frac{1}{2}h(\nu_H - \nu_D)$

4. Kinetic Salt Effect

Ions shield charges. How does adding salt ($NaCl$) affect rate?

Summary

Eyring Equation

$$k = \frac{k_B T}{h} e^{\Delta S/R} e^{-\Delta H/RT}$$

Tools for Mechanism:

  • Eyring Plot: Get $\Delta H^\ddagger, \Delta S^\ddagger$.
  • KIE: Is H-bond breaking the rate-limiting step?
  • Salt Effect: Are the reactants charged?
