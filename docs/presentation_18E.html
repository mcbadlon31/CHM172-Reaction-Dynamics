The Simplest Reaction:
Electron Tunneling

1. The Franck-Condon Principle

Fact: Electrons move ($10^{-16}$ s) much faster than nuclei ($10^{-13}$ s).

Consequence: ET occurs at fixed nuclear geometry.

  • Reactants must "distort" to a transition geometry before the electron jumps.
  • The energy cost to do this is $\lambda$ (Lambda).

2. Reorganization Energy ($\lambda$)

The energy required to rearrange the nuclei.

$$\lambda = \lambda_{in} + \lambda_{out}$$

Inner Sphere ($\lambda_{in}$)

Changes in bond lengths/angles of the reactants.

Outer Sphere ($\lambda_{out}$)

Reorientation of the solvent shell.

3. The Marcus Equation

Nobel Prize (1992). The activation energy depends on driving force ($\Delta G^\circ$).

$$\Delta G^\ddagger = \frac{(\Delta G^\circ + \lambda)^2}{4\lambda}$$

A parabola inside a parabola!

Rate Constant:

$$k_{ET} \propto \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta G^\ddagger}{RT}\right)$$

4. The Inverted Region

  1. Normal: $-\Delta G < \lambda$. Faster as $\Delta G$ drops.
  2. Barrierless: $-\Delta G = \lambda$. Maximum speed.
  3. Inverted: $-\Delta G > \lambda$. Slower as $\Delta G$ drops!

Counter-Intuitive!

Driving the reaction harder makes it go slower.

Biological Relevance

Photosynthesis

Goal: Separate charge ($D^+ \dots A^-$) to store energy.

Danger: Charge recombination (Back-reaction) wastes energy.

Solution:
Nature pushes the wasteful back-reaction into the Inverted Region so it becomes slow!

5. Distance Dependence

The electron tunnels through the medium.

$$k_{ET} \propto e^{-\beta r}$$
  • $\beta$: Decay constant (medium dependent).
  • $r$: Separation distance.
  • Effective only over short range ($< 15$ Å).

  • Inverted Region: Too much driving force slows you down.
